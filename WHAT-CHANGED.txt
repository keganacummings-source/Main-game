HOW THE GROUND WORKS
--------------------

ONE WorldData the size of the whole chart. Every position in the game (hunter,
foes, campfires, nodes, saves, the chart menu) is a chart position, so nothing
is ever translated between a region and the world. Only the 256 x 256 regions
near the hunter are built; every other tile is undefined, and blocked() reads
that as solid. A region is built when the hunter is within 96 tiles of it and
dropped when she is 160 away, so crossing a seam back and forth costs nothing.
Memory stays at four to nine regions however far you walk (about 155 MB heap in
a long full-size walk). Crossing a seam costs 15 to 60 ms once the code is warm.

The chart is built in a Web Worker (src/game/chart-worker.ts). It takes about
nine seconds, so a "Charting a new world…" screen is up for that long, once per
world. If a Worker is not available the chart is built in place instead: slow,
but the world is never the wrong shape.


ENEMIES — THE PART TO WATCH
---------------------------

A region is only terrain, so it brings no foes. chart-world.ts lays camps on
every region as it is built, using the same recipe the generated worlds use:
a 22-tile grid, a clean 10 x 10 square of open ground per cell, a pool of foes
that belong to the biome, two or three per camp (animals three), a respawn of
45-105 seconds. Each foe is spelled out as the spawn expandZones() would have
made (kind "mob", fixedLevel, zone with levels / species / respawn), so the
engine's proximity spawning, 45% random swap, respawn timers and kill credit
treat them like any other camp. Nothing in the fight code changed.

Levels come from distance to the start, since there is one continent and not
one per tier: 1-4 at the hearth, rising to about 70-105 at the far edge.
No camp within 22 tiles of the hearth or 8 tiles of any town, shrine, tavern or
door. Foes only stand on ground a hunter can walk on.

The engine keeps its list of spawn points in step with the regions
(applyStream): new ground adds its camps, dropped ground removes them, and a
foe that died and is waiting to respawn leaves with its ground rather than
ticking on forever. World bosses and bounty hunters belong to no region and are
never dropped. A region built again comes back identical.

Tested: every foe is on walkable ground, inside its own camp, in its level
range, with unique ids; the reported changes always add up to the real spawn
list across a long walk; the real applyStream() is cut out of engine.ts and run
against a stand-in to prove dormant foes follow the regions.


SITES AND RESOURCES
-------------------

The hearth, townsfolk, shrines, taverns, dungeon doors and the three world
bosses are re-homed around the start (townsfolk close in, the rest spread out
about ten tiles per old tile), each snapped to ground reachable on foot from
the start, so none is on an island or inside a mountain. Trees are real
choppable nodes (capped at 320 per region so the node list stays small; the
rest stay as walkable scenery), plus ore, mushrooms and fishing spots by biome.


WHO GETS IT
-----------

New characters and every rebirth after dying (unless "Keep Current World" is
ticked). Characters made before this keep the world they were rolled until
they die, because their campfires, logs and open rifts are pinned to it.
Loading a save, creating a character and rebirth all wait for the chart first.

A different seed's chart fog is never shown on a world (the save stores the
seed with it). Inside a dungeon the chart menu steps aside.

--------------

THE SHAPE OF THE WORLD
----------------------

Was: five landmasses spaced around a wide ring, which gave a scattered,
archipelago-ish chart with no centre to it and no sense of a homeland.

Now: ONE continent in the middle of an ocean.

It is built from a broad core at the centre plus a handful of LOBES overlapping
it. Because the lobes sit inside the core's reach they fuse with it rather than
standing apart, and what they add is shape — headlands, bays, peninsulas, the
occasional near-separated limb. The `continents` option is now how many lobes to
hang off the core.

An edge fade keeps the sea at the margins, so the continent sits IN an ocean
rather than running off the side of the chart.

Seed 2026: one landmass of 2,124,384 tiles, 117 named places, 34% land.


FOUR THINGS THAT HAD TO CHANGE WITH IT
--------------------------------------

1. CLIMATE IS MEASURED ACROSS THE LAND, NOT THE CHART.

   With everything in the middle of the chart, the whole continent sat in the
   tropics on chart latitude. The world came out with 22 tiles of taiga and 249
   of glacier — the cold biomes existed only because the guarantee pass forced
   them in. The gradient now spans the continent's own north-south reach, so it
   has poles at its top and bottom edges and an equator across its middle.

   Taiga 2.0%, glacier 1.8%, tundra 0.3% — naturally, before any guarantee.

2. LAKES ARE CARVED, NOT LEFT TO CHANCE.

   Lakes used to be whatever the coastline happened to strand, which on a single
   central continent is almost nothing: a few hundred tiles in a whole world. A
   continent this size should have real water in it, so basins are now carved
   inland — checked against a ring of land so a "lake" cannot open onto the sea
   and turn out to be a bay. Carved before the distance fields, so lakeshores
   get the damp they should and the ocean flood fill still cannot reach them,
   which is what marks them as lakes rather than sea.

   15,339 lake tiles on seed 2026, up from 384.

3. SETTLEMENTS SCALE WITH AREA.

   The old formula was tuned when places were shared between five continents.
   With one landmass it gave NINETEEN settlements for the entire world. One per
   ~18,000 tiles now, with spacing that scales to match, so they sit a
   reasonable walk apart: 117 places.

4. THE SHELF IS WIDER AND THE DISTANCE FIELDS ARE ROUND.

   A narrow shelf left the ocean as one flat expanse of deep blue right up to
   the shore, so the chart had no readable edge to the land. Widened.

   That exposed a second thing: the shallows gave out along an angular,
   diamond-shaped boundary. Both distance fields were plain four-neighbour
   chamfers, which measure Manhattan distance, and Manhattan contours ARE
   diamonds. They now use a 3-4 chamfer — an orthogonal step counts 3, a
   diagonal 4 — which approximates true distance closely enough for the
   contours to come out round.


TESTS UPDATED
-------------

Four tests asserted the old shape and now assert the new one. None was weakened
to pass — each says what it checks and why:

  - "continents are real landmasses" now requires the main continent to hold
    more than 15% of the chart, i.e. that the world really is centred on it.
  - "a full 2500 x 2500 world" requires a central continent over a million
    tiles rather than "more than one continent".
  - "every real landmass carries every land biome" needs one big landmass, not
    two.
  - the lake test now checks a real count AND that no lake reaches the edge of
    the chart, which is where the open sea is — a carved basin that opened onto
    the ocean would otherwise pass as a lake.
